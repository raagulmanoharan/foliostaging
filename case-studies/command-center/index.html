Command Center

Connecting signals. Understanding risk. Acting sooner.

A shared operational workspace for Salesforce Industries. I led the runtime design and helped teams explore and adopt the framework.

One industry’s need. A shared capability.

Salesforce’s industry teams build specialized solutions on the core platform. My team, Common Services, builds the tools and frameworks they share.

Life Sciences needed a command center. Regional leaders were tracking signals across systems and wanted to spot performance risks before they affected quarterly goals. Understanding a trend, investigating its causes and coordinating a response happened in different places.

We brought Life Sciences in as a design partner and framed the opportunity more broadly: a reusable operational workspace that other industry teams could configure for their own users.

The work, from first glance to next action.

The landing experience, alerts and watchlist form one journey. Design-time tools let industry teams define what that experience monitors.

Key design moments.

01Balance noise with insight.

A wall of equal-weight charts leaves the person to decide what matters. I gave the digest, alerts and watchlist distinct jobs: summarize the situation, surface meaningful exceptions and support ongoing attention.

Digest

What changed while I was away?

A short briefing

Alerts

What needs my attention?

Prioritized intervention

Watchlist

What am I keeping an eye on?

Ongoing monitoring

Foundational research identified fragmented signals and alert fatigue. Playing a day forward in the simulator also exposed more alerts than someone could reasonably review.

02Design the path between screens.

I treated the experience as a sequence of decisions. Home provides orientation; an alert holds the record; investigation keeps the original context nearby. Broader questions have room to expand without turning every task into one long conversation.

The supplied evaluation notes describe people losing the starting chart in long threads. Keeping the initiating card in view helped preserve the subject of the investigation.

03Make the answer something people can check.

An operational recommendation needs a basis someone can inspect. I brought the explanation, contributing factors and supporting evidence into a consistent alert structure, with human review before a proposed action changes data.

The evaluation identified visual validation, sources, explanation, override and an audit trail as trust drivers. These findings supported an inspectable record alongside conversation.

04Understand the request before replacing the interface.

Customers called the dashboard dated and asked for something conversational. I questioned whether that meant removing its immediate visibility. We explored the alternatives with customers and found a clearer requirement: show important signals by default, then let people investigate flexibly.

Research supported the hybrid direction: people valued conversation but needed visible context to know what to ask. The team took that approach to pilot.

05Design the setup behind the experience.

A shared framework also needs a design-time experience. Industry teams must be able to define alerts for their own domain while the runtime stays coherent. Alert setup connects what builders configure with what operational users eventually see.

06Extend the framework beyond the workspace.

Headless setup for forward-deployed engineers belongs here as a separate design moment.

Give teams something they can run.

The framework had to work for the teams adopting it, too. I built a sandbox and playground with sample industry data and a simulator so partners could experience changing conditions, try their own scenarios and discuss the same working artifact.

Shared patterns, setup guidance and extension rules gave teams a starting point they could build on. The playground made both the experience and its constraints easier to evaluate together.

From a shared direction to a pilot.

Dreamforce

The work reached the Dreamforce stage, making the Command Center experience tangible beyond the core project team.

Pilot

The team took the hybrid experience to pilot, carrying forward the relationship between visible signals and contextual investigation.

Pilot results are not available for this case study. Adoption, operational improvements and business impact are not claimed here.

Make the problem—and the trade-offs—clear.

The work connected two needs: helping operational users make informed decisions, and giving industry teams a framework they could reuse.

My role was to bring questions and a point of view early, use artifacts to make alternatives concrete, and refine decisions with research, product and engineering. The system became clearer when we designed the relationships between its parts, not just the screens.
